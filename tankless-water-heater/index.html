Flush and descale: why scale builds up and how the flush works

Heating water makes dissolved minerals drop out as scale, and a tankless heat exchanger has narrow passages that scale clogs. The Grand Rapids water system, which supplies Allendale Township, reports a 2025 plant-tap average of 8.3 grains per gallon (142 mg/L). That is a system average, not a reading at your tap, so test your tap before you decide how often to flush.

Scale shows up as swinging temperatures, weaker flow, kettle-like noises or a scale or overheat code. A vinegar flush circulates a mild acid through the exchanger to dissolve it, then rinses clean. The steps look like this at a high level.

Shut it down.

Turn off the power and gas, and close the isolation valves on the water lines.

Connect and circulate.

Hoses go on the service ports, and a small pump moves white vinegar through the exchanger.

Drain and rinse.

The vinegar is drained, the unit is rinsed with clean water and the inlet filter is cleaned.

Restore and test.

Valves reopen, the air is purged, the unit is powered up and checked for leaks and a steady temperature.

When to call instead of flushing it yourself

Call us if your unit has no isolation valves or service ports, since adding them is a plumbing job. Call if you are unsure about a gas unit, if a scale code returns soon after a flush, or if anything leaks, which is a water heater repair job. A flush done without closing the gas is a safety risk.

Landlords with units in rentals near the GVSU campus should schedule flushes on a calendar rather than waiting for a complaint. Tenants rarely know that a tankless heater needs descaling, and a unit that is ignored will often announce itself with a cold shower.

Gas line and venting: the part that surprises people

A tankless gas unit fires hard when it runs, so a gas line sized for a tank burner can be too small. Venting is different too. Many units need a dedicated exhaust, often plastic, plus a condensate drain, with clearances to windows and the ground. An electric unit may need a heavy circuit and a look at the panel.

In many houses, the support work costs more than the heater. If a tank is working and the supporting work will be large, we will tell you that staying with a tank is the better buy. For a broader look at the planned route, see water heater installation.

Error codes and what they usually point to

Codes differ by maker, so check the manual. These are the common families, not a brand guide.

Ignition or no flame.

Gas supply, igniter, flame sensor or a blocked vent can stop the burner from lighting.

Overheat or high limit.

Scale, low flow or a sensor fault can push the exchanger past its limit.

Vent or exhaust fault.

A blocked, disconnected or iced-over vent trips the safety circuit.

Low flow or flow sensor.

A clogged inlet filter, partly closed valve or failed sensor can keep the unit from starting.

Scale or maintenance.

The unit is telling you the exchanger needs a flush.

Frequently asked questions

Does my tankless water heater need flushing?

Usually, yes. Minerals form scale inside the heat exchanger. Ask your water utility about hardness and test your tap, then flush on a schedule that matches what you find.

Can I flush it myself with vinegar?

Some owners do, provided the unit has isolation valves and service ports and they shut off the gas and power first. If the valves are missing or you feel unsure, call us.

Why does my tankless heater give lukewarm water?

Scale, a clogged inlet filter, low flow or a failing sensor are common causes. The error code on the display helps narrow it down.

Is a tankless heater right for a house with an existing gas tank?

Only if the gas line and vent can handle it. We check both before quoting and say so if a tank is the better choice.

Can I put a tankless heater where the old tank sat?

Sometimes. The vent route, gas line and wall space decide it. We look at the site before giving you a price.

What do the error codes mean?

They vary by maker. Generally they point to ignition, overheating, venting, flow or scale. Read us the code and we will tell you what to check.

Do you repair tankless units that leak?

Yes. A leak can come from a fitting, a heat exchanger crack or a condensate line. Shut off the water to the unit, turn off the power and call us.
