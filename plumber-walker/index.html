Older stock, older wear points

Roughly 45% of the city's homes predate 1980 and about 16% predate 1960. Supply piping, shutoff valves and tanks in those houses have simply logged more years. When a basement pipe weeps or a fixture valve will not close, repiping is often the honest conversation once the repairs start stacking up.

With 65.2% of homes owner-occupied and 34.8% rented, calls split between owners and tenants waiting on a landlord. If water is running, close the main first. A tank that is rusting at the seams belongs on the water heater replacement list, not the repair list.

What to do with this: call us when a valve sticks, a joint weeps or a tank shows rust, and ask for the repair cost and the replacement cost side by side. Keep a record of each repair for your own planning and for any insurance claim. Do not wait for a full failure, because the repair that costs a little today can turn into a flooded basement.

The river edge: about 1,280 acres of FEMA Zone A

The city's parks plan says about 1,280 acres of Walker fall in FEMA Zone A, mostly along the Grand River at Millennium Park and the northeastern border. New flood maps took effect on 23 February 2023. We found no reported flood event, so this is about readiness, not history.

If your address is near the river, a sump pump that has sat unused since the thaw deserves a bucket test. Pour water into the pit until the float lifts, and listen for the pump to run and the pit to empty. A pump that hums or never stops needs sump pump work before the next heavy rain.

Grand Rapids water, Grand Rapids bill

Walker has no water or sewer utility of its own. The city says both systems are served entirely by the City of Grand Rapids, and that Walker is a customer community. Grand Rapids collects the bills, and Walker steps in only after 90 days unpaid.

Walker publishes no hardness figure. The Grand Rapids system reports a 2025 plant-tap average of 8.3 grains per gallon, but that is a plant number, so test your own tap. The city also says an independent plumber runs the lateral from the building to the edge of the right-of-way, which is where water service line repair comes in.

Permits stay local, and so do some wells

Unlike Allendale, where a contractor inspector handles permits, Walker runs its own Community Development department at 4243 Remembrance Rd NW. Plumbing applications go through its online portal or on paper. Ask the department about fees before you plan a job.

The city says not all parcels are hooked to public water and sewer, and a property sale does not force a connection. Kent County Health Department administers wells and septic in Walker. If you are unsure, ask the county about your parcel.

Roads and places we steer by

M-45 (Lake Michigan Dr) runs west to east through the city, and it is four lanes between Walker and GVSU, which is most of our drive. Remembrance Road holds City Hall and the Walker Ice and Fitness Center. Millennium Park and Harry A. Johnson Park, both county parks, sit at the river edge. Name the nearest one when you call.

For other Grand River-side addresses, see Marne to the west, or Jenison to the southwest.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency service available in Walker?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes Walker and Standale calls at any hour, and a plumber is sent east from Allendale. Say what is leaking and whether the main is closed. If you smell gas, get out and call 911 first.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Walker?

The city's own addresses, including City Hall on Remembrance Road, use 49534, and that is the ZIP we list. Walker has no ZIP named for it, and mail on the east side may show another Grand Rapids ZIP. Give the dispatcher your street address and we will place you in Walker or Standale.

How far is Walker from Allendale?

By our estimate about 10 miles and 15 minutes, east on M-45 and over the Grand River into Kent County. Standale is closer at about 8 miles and 10 minutes. Peak traffic adds time and we make no arrival promise.

Is Walker water hard?

Walker publishes no figure of its own. The Grand Rapids system, which supplies the city, reports an average of 8.3 grains per gallon at its plant tap for 2025. Your tap can differ, so test it before sizing a softener.

Who do I call about the water main in my Walker street?

The City of Grand Rapids runs and maintains both water and sewer for Walker. Walker is a customer community and the bills come from Grand Rapids. Anything inside your walls is a plumber's job, so close the main and call our line.

Who issues a plumbing permit in Walker?

The city's Community Development department does, through an online portal or a paper form. We did not find the plumbing fee, so ask the department directly.

Does a Zone A address change my plumbing needs?

It makes a working sump pump more important. About 1,280 acres of Walker are in FEMA Zone A, mostly near the river. For a pit that fills often, ask about basement waterproofing as well.

Are any Walker homes on wells or septic?

Some may be. The city says not all parcels are connected to public water and sewer, and Kent County Health Department administers wells and septic. Ask the county about your parcel.
