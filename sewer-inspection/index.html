Sewer Camera Inspection in Allendale, MI

A sewer camera inspection sends a small camera on a flexible cable through your house line, so the next decision rests on what is in the pipe instead of a guess. It is a planned visit. If sewage is already coming up, that is an emergency and the 24/7 line sends help for sewer line cleaning first.

What the camera shows

The camera head has lights and goes in through a cleanout, a pulled toilet or another access point. A screen shows the pipe wall as the camera moves along. We watch for cracks, broken or missing sections, joints that have slipped, roots, scale, grease, low spots where water sits and fittings that do not belong.

The cable has distance marks, so we know how far into the line each problem sits. Ask when you call whether a locating transmitter can be used, since knowing where your own pipe runs matters before any digging starts.

When to book an inspection

Backups that keep coming back.

After a second or third clog on the same line, stop cleaning and look.

Buying a home.

A crack or root intrusion shows up in minutes, and the pipe underground is the one part a walk-through cannot judge.

Before lining or digging.

A repair plan needs a picture of the damage, its length and the pipe's shape.

Smell or soggy ground.

A sewer odor in the yard or a wet patch with no rain can point to a leak in the line.

What a camera can and cannot do

Limits to know

  • It cannot see through a full blockage. The line has to be cleared enough for the camera to pass..
  • It sees the pipe at that moment, not how it will behave in ten years..
  • It cannot see inside walls above the floor or any pipe it cannot reach..
  • A line full of water or debris hides the pipe wall..

What it does well

  • It finds where and how far into the line a problem sits..
  • It tells a clog from a damaged pipe..
  • It shows whether lining will fit or a dig is needed..
  • It gives you facts to compare repair options..

Allendale: young homes, rural lines and rentals near GVSU

Allendale housing is young, with a median build year of 2003 and about 32 percent of units built since 2010. On a newer house a camera is more likely to turn up a low spot or construction debris than rusted iron. Older farmhouses in the rural parts are a different story and can hold clay or other old pipe. In Hudsonville, where the city plan says about half the housing predates 1979, expect the opposite.

If you are buying a rental near Grand Valley State University, remember that about 47 percent of households in the Allendale CDP rent. A house with years of tenant use can have wipe and grease damage that the seller does not know about. Ask for a camera look in the inspection window and ask what the visit includes.

On a rural property with septic, Ottawa County requires sellers to evaluate the septic and well before transfer. That evaluation is separate, and our camera covers the line between the house and the tank, not the tank itself.

After the inspection: what usually comes next

A clean pipe with no damage means you can stop worrying and keep cleaning on a schedule. A greasy or scaled pipe may call for hydro jetting, and a recurring clog in a sound pipe is a drain cleaning habit problem. A cracked, offset or collapsed section points to sewer line repair, and we show you the picture that supports it.

We do not sell repairs the camera does not support. If the pipe is fine, we say so, even when you were hoping for an answer to a recurring clog. Then the next question is what is going down the drain, not the pipe.

What affects the price of a camera inspection

A camera visit is priced by the work involved. These factors matter most.

Access.

A cleanout makes it simple. Pulling a toilet or digging out a buried cap adds time.

Line length.

A long run to a septic tank takes longer than a short run to the street.

Cleaning first.

If the line is blocked or heavy with grease, it has to be cleared before the camera can pass.

What you want recorded.

Ask what the visit includes, such as notes, locating or recorded footage.

Timing.

Weekend and after-hours visits are priced differently from a weekday booking.

Frequently asked questions

What does a sewer camera inspection show?

It shows cracks, breaks, roots, scale, grease, low spots and slipped joints, with distance marks. It tells a clog from a damaged pipe.

Should I get one before buying a home?

If the home is older, has big trees near the line, or is a rental with heavy use, yes. In a newer house the odds of a problem are lower, but the pipe is still the one part you cannot see, so it is worth asking about.

Can the camera pass through a blocked line?

No. The line has to be cleared enough for the camera to move. If it is fully blocked, we clear it first, and that may mean a drain cleaning call.

Will I get a report or video?

Ask what the visit includes when you call. We tell you plainly what we can provide and do not promise more than that.

How long does an inspection take?

It depends on how easy the access is and how long and clean the line is. We give you a realistic expectation when you call.

Does the camera inspect my septic tank?

No. It covers the line from the house to the tank. The tank itself is evaluated by other trades, and Ottawa County has its own evaluation rule at sale.

Can the camera find where my line runs?

Ask when you call whether a locating transmitter can be used on your visit. Private lines are not marked by MISS DIG 811, so knowing the path helps before any digging.

Call and tell us what is happening. We will explain what the visit covers before you book.
