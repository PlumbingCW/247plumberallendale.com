First, who owns which stretch of pipe

The Water & Sewer Department belongs to the city, with a billing clerk and published rates. Its 2025 report shows 1,455 service connections, no lead reported, and a material nobody has confirmed on 492. Anything found to be lead or galvanized gets replaced whenever the city tackles a capital project.

According to the 2024 report, the owner is responsible for the pipe on the house side of the curb stop. If a line from the street to your house is leaking, or you simply want to know what it is made of, the city can share what it has on file, and water service line repair covers the work on your side.

Third, an old grid with newer streets built outward

The city's draft master plan says homes in the central residential district date to the late 1890s and early 1900s. By its 2023 count, 263 of 1,755 homes (about 15%) predate 1940, roughly 30% predate 1960, and about 39% went up between 1990 and 2009, with a median year built of 1983.

The laterals under the oldest grid streets have had the longest to wear, so that is where we look first when one house backs up and its neighbors drain fine. Newer streets are being built east of the school campus. For the old core we start with sewer line repair, and with drain cleaning when the blockage is soft and recent.

Fourth, the sewer work the city already has planned

A contractor cleans the city's sanitary lines every year, and the 2026 round was announced for May 11 to June 30. The draft master plan also lists a Danforth Street sewer repair for 2026. If only your house backs up, the blockage is in your own lateral and that is our side of the work. If the whole street gurgles, call the city.

Fifth, the creek, the river and the towns we do not page

Deer Creek flows through the city. A 1982 FEMA study cited in the master plan says flooding is mostly confined to its undeveloped floodplain, with little historic damage. A wet basement in town is therefore worth checking for a failed pump or a blocked drain before blaming the creek. The Grand River is about 3.5 miles south of the city.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency service available in Coopersville?

Yes, around the clock. Nights, weekends and holidays go to the same dispatcher, who sends a plumber north from Allendale. Say what failed and whether the main is off. A gas smell means leave the house and call 911 first.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Coopersville?

Coopersville has one ZIP, 49404, and it reaches well past the city. It spans about 62 square miles against the city's roughly 5, and includes the Eastmanville area down by the Grand River. An address in Polkton Township carries the same ZIP as Main Street, and we dispatch to all of them.

Do you cross the Grand River to reach Coopersville?

Yes. The mapped route uses 68th Avenue, which crosses the river at Eastmanville, and takes about 15 minutes by estimate for roughly 8 miles. We do not promise an arrival time, and the dispatcher will give you a realistic one.

Is Coopersville water hard?

The city's 2024 and 2025 reports print no hardness figure. Grand Rapids measured 142 mg/L at its own plant, but that is not a Coopersville tap reading, so we will not quote it as one. A test strip or a call to the Water & Sewer Department gives you the real number.

Who owns the water line to my house?

The city's 2024 report puts the customer side of the curb stop on the owner, so the stretch from the stop to your house is yours. The city says it replaces lead and galvanized lines during its own projects. Ask what its inventory shows for your address.

Who handles plumbing permits in Coopersville?

The city's Building Department page says the city enforces state building codes and handles plumbing permits through its contract inspector, Professional Code Inspectors. The page we read gave no plumbing fees, so ask the department directly.

Do you serve Grand Haven or Spring Lake?

Neither has its own page here. They are real towns in Ottawa County, and we have not mapped a drive time for either, so call the line and the dispatcher will say whether we can come.

Only my Coopersville house backs up. Whose problem is that?

Usually yours. If neighbors drain fine, the blockage is in your own lateral, and drain cleaning is the first step. If a camera shows damage, we discuss repair before anything else.
