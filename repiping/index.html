Repiping in Allendale, MI

Repiping means replacing the water supply pipes in a house, and sometimes the drains too. It is a planned job, not a reflex. If a pipe is spraying or flooding right now, that is a burst pipe repair call to the emergency line, which is open 24/7.

This page covers the decision itself: whether your pipes are failing, which material fits, and how to do it with the least damage to your house.

Does a young town like Allendale need repiping?

Usually not, and we would rather say so. Allendale's housing is young. The median home was built in 2003, about 16 percent of units predate 1980 and only about 3.5 percent predate 1940. Most plumbing problems here are fixture and connection failures, not worn-out piping.

Repiping is mainly a question for older homes, rural farmhouses and the nearby towns with older stock. If you own a house built long before 2003, or one in Walker, Jenison or Marne, the age of the pipe is worth a conversation. Our Walker plumber page covers the east side of the river.

Is galvanized pipe failing? How to tell

Galvanized steel pipe has a zinc coating. Over decades the zinc wears away and the steel rusts, mostly on the inside, where nobody can see it. Rust and mineral buildup narrow the pipe, so flow drops long before anything leaks.

Weak flow at several taps.

Pressure is fine at the meter but poor in the house, especially at the farthest fixtures.

Brown or rust-colored water.

Especially on the first draw after the water sat overnight.

A magnet sticks.

Steel pipe attracts a magnet. Copper and plastic do not. It is a simple way to know what you have.

Flaking at joints.

Rust bloom around threaded fittings, or leaks that return after a repair, are late signs.

PEX versus copper: how the two compare for a repipe

Both are good replacement materials. The right one depends on the house and on your priorities.

Copper pipe questions

Copper is a proven material, and if a house already has it, replacement is not automatic. Many copper systems run for decades. What changes the answer is a pattern: pinholes at several spots along a run, green staining at joints, or repeated leak repairs. One pinhole is a repair. Three in a year is a pipe wearing out.

Cast iron drain lines

Water supply is only half the picture. In older houses, the drains can be cast iron, which rusts from the inside out, develops rough scale that catches paper and grease, and can crack at the hubs or sag where a support has shifted. Slow drains in several fixtures and repeated clogs in the main are the usual clues.

Before anyone proposes pulling out a drain stack, a camera inspection shows the actual condition. Our sewer line inspection page explains what the camera sees and what it cannot. A split supply pipe on any material is a job for burst pipe repair, and a failing buried line is covered under water service line repair.

Phased repiping or the whole house at once

Phased repipe

  • Spreads cost and disruption across several visits..
  • Starts with the worst run, such as a bathroom group or the kitchen..
  • Leaves old and new pipe in one house, with fittings where the two meet..
  • Works well for rentals, where units turn over at different times..

Whole house at once

  • One disruption, one set of wall openings, one patching job..
  • Water is off for the work period, and we tell you how long before we start..
  • New piping throughout and one clean set of connections..
  • Usually the better value when most of the original piping is in poor shape..

Access and drywall: the honest version

Repiping a finished house means opening walls and ceilings in some places. How much depends on where the pipes run, how the house was framed and whether there is a basement, crawlspace or attic with open access. Flexible tubing needs fewer openings than rigid pipe, but it is never zero.

We tell you where the openings will be before work begins. Drywall patching and painting are often a separate trade, and we say so up front. For permits and inspections, the township uses Professional Code Inspections, so ask them, and us, what applies to your job.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my pipes need to be replaced?

The signs are weak flow at several taps, rusty water, repeated leaks and a pipe material known for age failure, such as galvanized steel. A magnet test and a pressure check give us a first answer on the phone.

Does Allendale have a lot of galvanized pipe?

We have no data on pipe materials in Allendale, and we will not guess. What we know is the age: the median home was built in 2003. Older homes and rural properties are where we look for galvanized steel.

Is PEX better than copper?

Neither is best for every house. PEX is flexible and usually less expensive. Copper is proven and does not mind sunlight. We explain which fits your walls and your water.

Can you repipe a few rooms instead of the whole house?

Yes. A phased repipe starts with the worst run and continues later. The tradeoff is that old and new pipe coexist, and we explain where the transitions will be.

Will you tear up my walls?

Some openings are unavoidable. We tell you where before work begins and keep them as small as the layout allows. Patching and painting are often handled by another trade.

Do I need a permit for repiping?

Ask the township's permit and inspection office, which is Professional Code Inspections, and ask us. Do not rely on a general answer, because it depends on the scope of the job.

What if a pipe bursts before I decide?

Close the main valve and call the emergency line. We handle burst pipes 24/7, and an emergency repair does not commit you to a repipe.
