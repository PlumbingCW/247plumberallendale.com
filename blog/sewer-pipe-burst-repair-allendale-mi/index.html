Sewer Pipe Burst Repair for Allendale Homes

A broken sewer pipe usually shows itself as a soggy or sunken patch of yard, several fixtures backing up at once, or a sewage smell outdoors. The first moves are to stop using water, keep people away from the wet ground, and call a plumber.

This guide covers the signs, what to do first, how digging and trenchless lining differ, MISS DIG 811 and permits, and the question of who owns which part of the line.

Signs a sewer pipe has failed

Soggy or sunken yard.

A wet patch that stays wet in dry weather, or ground that dips along the line, suggests water is escaping from the pipe.

Sinkhole.

Soil can wash into a broken pipe and leave a void. Treat a sudden hole as unstable ground and keep people and pets back.

Several fixtures back up.

If the toilet, tub and floor drain all misbehave together, the blockage or break is in the main line, not in one fixture.

Sewage smell outside.

A smell near the yard or foundation, with no sewage in the house, can point to a leak underground.

Greener, faster grass.

A strip of lush grass over the line can mean the soil is being fertilized by a leak.

What to do first

Stop using water.

No showers, laundry, dishwasher or flushing. Every gallon you send down goes into the break or into the house.

Keep clear of the wet ground.

Do not walk on sunken soil, and keep children and pets away. Avoid contact with sewage and wash your hands well if you touch anything wet.

Note what you see.

Write down where the wet spot is, when it started, and which fixtures misbehave. Photos help. Do not dig to look.

Call a plumber.

A camera inspection shows where and how the pipe failed. If sewage is coming into the house, say so on the call.

Digging or trenchless: the honest comparison

Nobody can choose a repair method before seeing the pipe. A sewer inspection with a camera shows the type of damage, how far it runs, how deep the pipe is and what sits above it. Then the choices become clearer.

Lining and pipe bursting avoid trenching but are not always possible. A collapsed pipe, a badly offset joint or a serious low spot that holds water usually means digging. A plumber who promises lining before looking at the pipe is guessing.

MISS DIG 811, permits and who owns which part

Call 811, the MISS DIG line, at least 3 business days before any digging, for any project regardless of size. It costs nothing. Utility locators mark public lines, but private lines such as a sewer lateral or a sump discharge are not marked, so your plumber locates your own line with a camera and locator.

Professional Code Inspections (PCI) handles plumbing permits and inspections for the township. Ask your plumber whether your repair needs a permit, and ask PCI about the process for your address.

Where the township's sewer ends and your responsibility begins is a question for the township utility. Ask it before you assume, ideally with camera findings in hand. We will not guess it for you.

Allendale, MI: the public system around your lateral

The township's sewer system includes a Water Resource Recovery Facility rated for 1.6 million gallons a day, with an expansion under way, about 50 miles of sewer main and 8 lift stations. Average flow at the plant runs about 1.3 million gallons a day when GVSU is in session and about 1.0 when it is out, which shows how much the campus population shapes load.

If neighbors on your street have backups at the same time, call the township utility's posted emergency number as well as a plumber.

Rural properties are different. Homes outside the sewered areas use private septic systems, and Ottawa County Health Department oversees well and septic matters. A soggy yard on a rural lot may be a septic problem, not a broken sewer pipe, and the county is the place to ask.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know whether the break is in my yard or in the street?

Where the sewage appears and which fixtures back up give clues, and a camera inspection shows the exact spot. Where the public line begins is a question for the township utility, so ask it with the findings in hand.

Can a broken sewer pipe be lined instead of dug up?

Sometimes. Lining needs a pipe that is still continuous and roughly round. A collapsed pipe or a badly offset joint usually needs digging, and only a camera inspection can say which you have.

Is it safe to keep using the toilet?

No. Until a plumber has looked at the line, avoid showers, laundry, dishes and flushing. Every gallon either leaks into the ground or backs up into the house.

Do I need to call MISS DIG 811 before a sewer repair?

Yes, for any digging. Call at least 3 business days ahead, for any project regardless of size. It costs nothing, and private lines are not marked, so your plumber locates your own sewer line.

Will insurance pay for a broken sewer line?

Policies differ. Sudden damage may be covered, while gradual wear and root damage often are not, and sewer line coverage is sometimes an optional add-on. Check your policy before work starts.

Does a sewer repair need a permit?

Ask your plumber and ask Professional Code Inspections, the firm that handles plumbing permits and inspections for the township. Do not assume the answer. It depends on the work and the address.

What if I live on a rural Allendale lot with a septic system?

A septic lot has no street sewer to back up into, so a soggy yard may be a tank or drain field problem. The Ottawa County Health Department oversees well and septic matters. Call a plumber to look at the house side of the line.
