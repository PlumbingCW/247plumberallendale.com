What a thermocouple does, in plain words

On a gas tank water heater with a standing pilot, the thermocouple is a short metal probe that sits in the pilot flame. The flame heats it, and it makes a tiny electrical signal that holds the gas valve open. If the flame goes out, the probe cools, the signal stops and the valve shuts off the gas.

That is why a bad one has such a typical story: the pilot lights while you hold the button, then dies a few seconds after you let go. Many newer heaters use electronic ignition and have no standing pilot, so the part may not exist on yours. Check the label or the manual.

Pilot won't stay lit? It is not always the thermocouple

Worn thermocouple.

The classic case. Probe tips wear and corrode over time, and the pilot drops out after you release the button.

Flame in the wrong place.

A weak flame, or one that does not wrap the probe tip, cannot heat it enough to hold the valve open.

Dirty pilot assembly.

Dust or soot in the pilot tube starves the flame. A steady blue flame looks healthy, while a yellow or flickering one calls for a service visit.

Draft or venting trouble.

A downdraft can blow the pilot out. If it goes out only when a fan or the furnace starts, tell us. This is a safety matter, because combustion gases may not be leaving the house.

Failing gas control valve.

If a new thermocouple does not help, the valve may not be holding open. That repair costs more and often moves the question toward replacement.

What drives the cost of a thermocouple job

We do not post dollar figures, because the number depends on your heater. These are the things that move the price, roughly in the order they matter. When you compare two quotes, ask each company to list the same things: the part, the visit, any other fixes and the tank's condition.

On insurance: sudden water damage from a failed tank is the usual covered event, while a worn part and routine maintenance usually are not. Check your own policy.

The visit and its timing.

A daytime visit and a night or weekend visit are not priced the same way. We offer same-day appointments when available.

The part itself.

A basic thermocouple is a small part. Some heaters use a thermopile or a model-specific assembly that costs more.

Access and condition.

A heater in a tight closet, a corroded connection or a pilot tube that needs cleaning adds time.

What else turns up.

A dirty pilot, a draft problem or a leaking gas fitting changes the scope. We tell you before doing more.

The age of the tank.

If the tank is near the end of its life, a new part may not be worth buying, and replacement is the fairer comparison.

Frequently asked questions

Is a thermocouple the same as a thermopile?

They are related but not the same. A thermocouple makes a small signal that holds the gas valve open, while a thermopile is a larger version that can power the valve controls. Which one you have depends on the heater model.

Can I replace a thermocouple myself?

It can look like a small job, but it involves a gas connection that must be leak-checked afterward. We would rather you call than guess about gas. If you smell gas at any point, leave and call your gas utility or 911.

Why does my pilot go out when I let go of the button?

That is the textbook thermocouple symptom, because the probe is not generating enough signal to hold the valve open. A weak flame, dirt or a draft can cause the same thing, so the part is not always to blame.

My pilot still goes out after a new thermocouple. What now?

The cause is likely elsewhere, such as the pilot assembly, a draft or the gas control valve. Do not keep relighting it. Call us so we can check the combustion side.

When is a new tank better than a repair?

When the tank is old, rusting at the base, leaking or rumbling, a new part only delays the bigger bill. We will compare repair and water heater replacement with you, and you hear the clear price before work starts.

How do I know if my heater has electronic ignition?

Look for a lighting instruction label and a pilot button. If there is no pilot window or button, and the unit has an ignition control or a display, it likely has no thermocouple. Your manual or the model number will confirm it.

Do I need a permit in Allendale to replace a gas water heater?

Ask Professional Code Inspections, which handles plumbing and mechanical permits for the township. A like-for-like replacement, a gas line change and a small part swap can be treated differently. We will tell you what we see on the call.
