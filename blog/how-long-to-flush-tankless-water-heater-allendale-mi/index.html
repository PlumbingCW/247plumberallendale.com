How Long to Flush a Tankless Water Heater (Allendale Homeowner Guide)

The honest answer is a range, and your unit's manual sets it. A flush is a circulation period with descaling solution through the heat exchanger, followed by a rinse. How long depends on the model, how much scale is in it and how you set up, so we will not print one number for every brand.

Below is the general process, how to tell the job is done, and how hard water fits in. Where this page and your manual disagree, follow the manual.

Why there is no single number

Tankless makers publish their own flush procedures, and the circulation time, the solution and the interval between flushes differ by model. A number that fits one brand could be wrong for another. Your manual, or the maker's support page for your exact model number, is the authority.

What changes the time: how much scale is on the heat exchanger, how strong the solution is, how big the pump and hoses are, and whether the unit already has service valves. For planning, allow a couple of hours for a first try, counting setup, circulation, draining and rinse. That is our own rough allowance and not a manufacturer figure.

The general process, step by step

This works only if your unit has service ports or isolation valves. If it does not, adding them is plumbing work, and that is a good time to call. Never mix vinegar with bleach or any other cleaner.

Power and gas off.

Switch off the unit's power, close the gas supply if it is a gas unit, then close the isolation valves on the hot and cold sides.

Connect hoses and pump.

Attach hoses to the service ports. Run one into a clean bucket and the other from a small pump sitting in the bucket with the solution your manual calls for, which may be white vinegar or a product the maker sells.

Circulate for the time in your manual.

Open the ports and run the pump so solution moves through the unit. The manual gives the time, often as a range. Do not cut it short and do not let the pump run dry.

Drain, rinse, clean the filter, restart.

Drain the solution, then rinse with clean water until the discharge runs clear. Clean the inlet screen, close the ports, open the isolation valves, bleed air at a hot tap, then restore gas and power.

How to tell the flush is done

Look for clear water, not the clock. The rinse is finished when the discharge runs clear, with no cloudy flecks and no vinegar smell, and the hot taps flow at their normal rate.

After the restart, the unit should fire and reach temperature without an error code. If you still see the same code, weak flow or cycling on and off, scale may not have been the cause. Check the aerators at the taps, then call us.

Allendale, MI: Grand Rapids water and your tap

Allendale Township buys its water from the City of Grand Rapids. The Grand Rapids system reports a 2025 plant-tap average hardness of 142 mg/L, or 8.3 grains per gallon. That is a system average, not a measurement at your faucet, and the township's own water quality report prints no hardness number. Use a test kit or ask your water utility for your own number, because it drives how fast scale builds.

Jenison and Hudsonville are on different suppliers, so do not carry the 8.3 figure to a home there. Parts of Allendale Township without municipal water use private wells and can differ either way, and the Ottawa County Health Department offers water testing.

Source: the City of Grand Rapids 2025 average water analysis, the township's 2025 water quality report, and Ottawa County well-water guidance.

Flush it yourself, or call a plumber?

If the test says your water is hard, a water softener or filter is the longer-term fix. Our tankless water heater service covers flushing, repairs and installs.

Reasonable to do yourself

  • The unit already has service valves.
  • You have the manual, a pump, hoses and a quiet afternoon.
  • No error codes or leaks to start with.

Better to call us

  • No service valves, which means installing them first.
  • A gas unit with any gas smell or ignition trouble.
  • An error code that returns after a flush.
  • A leak, or a rental unit that belongs to your landlord.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I flush a tankless water heater?

Check your manual, because makers set their own intervals and many tie them to water hardness and how much hot water the household uses. Harder water usually means flushing more often. When in doubt, ask for a test of your tap first.

Can I use plain white vinegar?

Many manuals allow it and some name a specific solution, so follow yours. Vinegar is an acid, so ventilate the room and never mix it with bleach or another cleaner. Rinse thoroughly afterward.

Do I need a pump?

Most procedures use a small submersible pump with hoses to push solution through the unit. Gravity alone does not move it through the heat exchanger well. Your manual lists what the maker expects.

What if my tankless water heater has no service valves?

Then adding them is a plumbing job, and it makes every future flush easier. Call us and we can talk it through. The price depends on your piping, and you hear it before work starts.

Will flushing clear an error code?

Sometimes, if the code relates to scale or flow. If the same code returns after a flush and a clean inlet screen, something else is wrong. Call us rather than resetting it again and again.

Can a water softener replace flushing?

A softener reduces scale, but it does not replace the maintenance steps in your manual. Ask the maker how the two fit together. A softener also needs its own upkeep.

Should I test my tap if I live in Allendale on Grand Rapids water?

Yes. The system average is a starting point, and your own number tells you how fast scale is likely to form. A test kit or your water utility can give you one.
