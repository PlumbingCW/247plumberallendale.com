When you need an installation, not a repair

A remodel is planned.

Moving a sink, adding a bathroom or opening a wall changes where water and drains must run. The pipes go in before the drywall.

An appliance has no hookup.

A washer, dishwasher or ice maker needs a valve, a drain and sometimes a dedicated line. A hose into a wall that was never plumbed for it is a leak waiting to happen.

A fixture is past saving.

Cracked porcelain, a corroded faucet body or a fixture with no available parts. See our pages on toilet installation and faucets for the details.

New building or addition.

New walls need rough-in, top-out and finish stages, each with an inspection.

Gas safety.

Gas smell? Leave first, then call

If you smell gas, do not stop to call us. Safety comes before any plumbing visit, and we never promise a response time on a gas leak.

  1. Leave the building right away with everyone inside, and leave the door open behind you..
  2. Do not use light switches, phones, garage door openers or anything that can spark inside the house..
  3. From outside, call your gas utility or 911 first. They shut off gas and make the building safe..
  4. Call us only after the utility or emergency crew says the building is clear. We can then inspect or repair the piping..

Gas line inspection and installation, honestly described

A gas line job adds or extends pipe to a range, dryer, furnace, fireplace or outdoor appliance, fits a shutoff at the appliance, then tests the joints for leaks. An inspection checks existing piping, connectors and shutoffs and reports what looks wrong. We do not diagnose gas leaks over the phone and we do not work on a line while gas is escaping.

Utility gas heats about 5,900 homes in Allendale Township, according to the American Community Survey, so range, dryer and furnace connections are common. Plumbing permits and inspections for the township go through Professional Code Inspections, so ask them what your gas job needs before work begins.

Backflow protection and what a preventer does

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that stops water from running backward into the clean supply when pressure drops. It matters wherever a hose, a sprinkler system, a boiler or commercial equipment connects to drinking water, because the water behind the valve may have touched fertilizer, chemicals or soil.

Michigan has plumbing rules on backflow assemblies, including testing for some of them. Ask your inspector or water utility which devices apply to your property and whether they need to be tested. We install the assembly you are told you need. For businesses, see commercial plumbing.

New-construction plumbing, stage by stage

A new home or addition moves through three plumbing stages. Rough-in puts drain, vent and supply pipe in the framing. Top-out finishes the upper levels and tests the system. The final stage sets fixtures after the walls are finished. Each stage is a point where an inspector can sign off, so the plumber and builder schedule around it.

Frequently asked questions

What if I smell gas?

Leave the building, then call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not call us first and do not use switches or phones inside. We can inspect the piping after the utility says the building is safe.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in Allendale?

Professional Code Inspections issues the township's plumbing permits and does the inspections. Ask them whether your job needs one, because that depends on the work.

Why do I need to call 811 before digging?

MISS DIG 811 notifies utilities so they can mark buried lines. Call at least three business days ahead. It is free. Private lines such as a sump discharge or a sprinkler line are not marked, so ask us how we handle them.

Do you install backflow preventers?

Yes, the assembly you are told you need. Which devices apply to your property, and whether they must be tested, is a question for your inspector or water utility.

Can you hook up a gas range or dryer?

Yes where gas piping already exists. We fit a shutoff and an approved connector, then check for leaks. New gas runs are priced after we see the layout.
