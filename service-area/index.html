What sets each town apart

A quick look at the main difference, with a full page for each town.

Jenison

An unincorporated community in Georgetown Charter Township, about 10 miles away. The township buys its water from the City of Wyoming and its sewage is treated in Grandville. See plumber in Jenison.

Hudsonville

An incorporated city about 10 miles south. It buys finished water from the City of Wyoming through Ottawa County and sends its sewage to the Grandville plant. See plumber in Hudsonville.

Coopersville

A small city due north, about 8 miles away, with its own water and sewer department and Grand Rapids system water. See plumber in Coopersville.

Marne

An unincorporated community where utility service depends on the address, from township water to private wells and septic. See plumber in Marne.

Walker

A Kent County city east across the Grand River, about 10 miles away by M-45, on Grand Rapids water and sewer with its own permit office. See plumber in Walker.

How to read the town pages

Each town page covers the same core services but starts from local facts. You will find who supplies the water, which ZIP codes the page covers, what the housing is like and a short guide to what to do before we arrive. If you live between towns, read the page for the nearer one.

The road estimates on those pages are rough distances from our address. They are not arrival promises. Timing on any call depends on the job, the weather and where our technician is when you phone.

Rural addresses and private wells

Many addresses in the outer townships are not on municipal water or sewer. A home on a private well and septic system has different failure points from one on a city line, and some of the repairs belong to a well or septic specialist. We say so when that is the case.

Tell us your road and township when you call. It helps us judge whether the problem is inside the house, on the service line or out of our scope.

What we ask when you call from a new town

Give the street, the nearest cross road and the town name. Two Ottawa County addresses can share a ZIP code and still sit in different townships, which matters for who runs the water and sewer service. A quick answer on your side saves a callback.

If you are in Walker or another part of Kent County, say so. The utilities and permit offices are different from those in Ottawa County, and the right local contact for a municipal problem changes with it.

Emergencies outside normal hours

Night and weekend calls from any of these towns go to the same 24/7 line. If water is spreading, the first job is stopping it, and we walk you through the main shutoff before talking about anything else. After that we explain what can be done tonight and what can safely wait for daylight.

Sewage backups and gas smells are handled with extra care. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. We can help with the plumbing side once it is safe.
