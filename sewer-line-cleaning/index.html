Sewer backup: what to do first, and what to leave alone

  1. Stop all water use, including the washer, dishwasher and showers..
  2. Do not flush, and keep people and pets away from the water. Sewage can carry bacteria..
  3. Do not use drain cleaners, and do not pump the water out of the basement into the yard..
  4. If water has reached outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay out of the room and call an electrician. Do not walk through standing water to reach a switch..
  5. Call us. Tell us which drains are backing up and whether any neighbors have the same problem..

Sewer backup: the main line or your own line?

This is the first question we ask on every backup call. The public sewer main under the street is run by the township utility. Your house line, the pipe from the foundation out to that connection, serves one home. We clear that one.

Clues point one way or the other. If only your house is backing up, the blockage is likely in your line. If neighbors are reporting the same problem, or you hear the street manhole gurgle, the main may be the cause. Call the township utility, which posts separate daytime and after-hours emergency numbers on its water and sewer page.

Where one stops and the other begins is a question for the utility. Ask the township utility where its responsibility ends before you assume either way, then call us for what is on your side.

What not to flush or pour into a sewer line

Wipes labeled flushable still do not break down like paper. These are the common causes of repeat blockages.

  • Wipes of any kind, including baby, cleaning and makeup wipes.
  • Paper towels, tissues, cotton swabs and dental floss.
  • Cooking grease, fat and oil, even when warm.
  • Feminine products and diapers.
  • Coffee grounds, rice and pasta in the kitchen line.
  • Cat litter, which sets like cement in a pipe.

Homes on septic: what we clear, and what we do not

In the rural south and west of the township, many homes sit on private septic regulated by the Ottawa County Health Department. For these homes we clear the line between the house and the tank. We do not pump tanks. Tank pumping is a separate trade, and a licensed septic hauler does it.

The signs differ. A line blockage usually shows up as one or two drains failing. A full tank tends to make every drain slow and sometimes leaves a wet, foul-smelling patch in the yard. If you are not sure, ask when you call. We will tell you honestly which one it sounds like instead of sending a truck for the wrong job.

Septic repairs need county permits, so ask the health department before any digging. Neighboring Marne is a mix of sewer and septic too, and the same question applies there.

Main sewer line drain cleaning

The main drain is the 4 inch pipe that collects every branch in the house and leaves through the foundation toward the street. We reach it through a cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the basement, crawl space or yard, and run a cable or a jetter down it.

If the cable returns roots, scale or wipes, that tells us what we are dealing with. If it will not pass a certain point, we recommend a sewer camera inspection before repeating the job.

Allendale's sewer system and where your side begins

The township sewer system has about 50 miles of main, 8 lift stations and a treatment facility. A problem at a lift station or in the main can look like a block-wide backup, so ask the neighbors before you assume the fault is on your property.

Rural homes outside the sewer service area use private septic, with the Ottawa County Health Department overseeing wells and septic systems. If you are not sure which system serves your address, the township utility can tell you, and so can your closing paperwork.

How we clear a backed-up sewer line

Find the cleanout.

We locate it or make an access point, so the cable goes into the line rather than through the house.

Cable the line.

A drum cable machine with a cutter head sized to the pipe goes down until it finds the blockage.

Jet if needed.

Grease or scale may call for hydro jetting after the cable opens the line.

Run water and report.

We flush the line, test fixtures and tell you if repeated backups call for a camera.

What changes the price of clearing a sewer line

A backup call can be a quick cable run or a long job. These factors set the number.

Cleanout access.

Having one speeds the job. Without it, we may need to pull a toilet or create access.

Length of the line.

A short run to the street is simpler than a long run to a rear septic tank.

What is blocking it.

Roots and scale take more effort than a wipe clog.

Tools.

Cabling, jetting and a camera each add time, and you hear the plan first.

Time of day.

Nights, weekends and holidays carry a different rate. We tell you before work begins.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle sewer line emergencies at night or on weekends in Allendale?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, nights and weekends included. Call, tell us which drains are backing up, and we explain what to do while we get there. The price is given before work starts.

Is a sewer backup my responsibility or the township's?

It depends on where the blockage is. We clear the house line. For the public main in the street, ask the township utility where its responsibility ends, and call its emergency line if neighbors are affected too.

Can I use my basement bathroom after a backup?

No. Stop flushing and running water until the line is cleared, because any water going down adds to the flooding.

Do you pump septic tanks?

No. We clear the line from the house to the tank. Pumping a tank is a separate trade, so ask when you call and we will say whether it sounds like a line or a tank.

How do I clean up after sewage reaches the floor?

Wear gloves and boots, ventilate, and keep children out. Porous items like carpet and drywall can hold contamination, so check with your insurer or a restoration company before tearing out anything.

Why does my sewer line keep backing up?

Repeat backups usually mean roots, a sagging section or a damaged joint. After the second one, ask for a sewer line repair opinion based on a camera look.

Is there a way to prevent sewer backups?

Keep wipes, grease and paper towels out, and have the line cleaned if roots are known to be present. Some homes also benefit from a backwater valve, but ask your inspector before adding one.
