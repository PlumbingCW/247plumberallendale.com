The parts that go with the heater: expansion tank, relief discharge, venting, gas

A water heater is more than the tank. A temperature and pressure relief valve needs a discharge pipe that runs safely to where hot water can do no harm. Where the system is closed, for example with a backflow device or pressure reducing valve on the incoming line, an expansion tank gives the heated water somewhere to go.

Gas units add a flue or vent run, a gas shutoff, a sediment trap and, for tankless, sometimes a larger gas line. Electric units add a breaker and wire sized to the elements. We walk the route from the old unit to the new one before we quote, because that is where surprises hide.

If a gas line needs to move or grow, our plumbing installation work covers it. If you want to compare on-demand heating more closely, the tankless water heater page goes deeper.

Four signs to plan an install before the tank decides for you

Mineral buildup speeds all four, so a water softener or filter is worth raising at install time.

Rust at the base or fittings.

Orange stains on the top connections or around the bottom mean moisture is working on the steel.

Rumbling or popping.

Sediment on the floor of the tank traps water that boils beneath it, and the noise follows.

Shorter showers.

Hot water that runs out faster than it used to suggests lost capacity or a failing element.

A damp drain pan.

A pan that is never quite dry can be the first warning of a slow leak.

What changes the cost of an installation

We give you a clear price before work starts and do not quote dollar figures online. Five things move the number most.

Type of heater.

A standard tank, a tankless unit and a heat pump hybrid have very different equipment and labor.

Fuel changes.

Switching between gas and electric adds a circuit or a gas line plus venting.

Venting.

A new flue or a plastic vent run through walls costs more than reusing a sound existing one.

Code items.

Expansion tank, discharge piping, pan, shutoff valves and sediment trap all show up on the list.

Access and finish work.

Condo closets, finished basements and long pipe runs add time.

Frequently asked questions

Should I choose a tank, tankless or a hybrid water heater?

A standard tank is the simplest swap. Tankless suits tight spaces and homes where several baths run together. A heat pump hybrid fits an electric home with open space around the unit.

What is an expansion tank and do I need one?

It is a small tank that absorbs the extra volume when water heats. Closed plumbing systems often need one. We check the incoming line and tell you before quoting.

Why does the relief valve need a discharge pipe?

If the valve ever opens, the pipe carries hot water or steam to a safe spot. It must stay open and unobstructed, and we do not cap or plug it.

Can I switch from electric to gas?

Often, if a gas line and a vent route can reach the spot. That makes it a bigger project than a swap, and we explain the extra work before you decide.

Who handles the permit and inspection?

Professional Code Inspections handles plumbing and mechanical permits and inspections for Allendale Township. Ask us what to expect and ask the inspector which code edition applies.

What should I do before the installers arrive?

Clear a path to the tank, have the old label ready and fill a few containers with water. If you rent, make sure the owner has approved the work.

Our home is new. When will we need to replace the heater?

It depends on the model and your water, so look at the manufacture date on the rating plate. Newer Allendale homes are generally years from a replacement, and a yearly flush helps.
