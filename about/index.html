How we work

Everything starts with a phone call. Plumbing problems are hard to judge from a form, and a two-minute conversation tells us more than a paragraph of typed notes. You describe what you see and hear, we ask a few questions, and you get a first step to limit damage before anyone drives to your door.

From there the job is scoped and priced by phone. You hear the clear price before work starts, and you decide. We offer same-day appointments when they are available, and we tell you the next opening when they are not.

Why Allendale

Allendale is a township of about 26,600 people (2020 Census, township Master Plan) built around a university campus, a commercial corridor on Lake Michigan Drive and a lot of new housing. The Master Plan reports 2,053 more housing units in 2020 than in 2010, and ACS data puts the median year a home was built at 2003.

That mix shapes the work. New homes bring fixture and water heater problems and the plumbing needs of rentals that change hands often. The rural edges bring private wells, septic systems and farmhouses with older pipes. We plan for both.

What we do not do

We do not repair the municipal water or sewer main under the street, and we do not pump septic tanks. If your problem turns out to belong to the township utility or to another trade, we will say so and point you to the right place rather than sell you work you do not need.

How to verify any plumber in Michigan

Michigan licenses plumbers through the Bureau of Construction Codes at the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA). The state recognizes four categories, and you can look any of them up before you hire.

  • Plumbing Apprentice Registration: a person learning the trade under supervision..
  • Journey Plumber License: 6,000 hours over at least three years as a registered apprentice, plus passing the written and practical exams..
  • Master Plumber License: at least 4,000 hours as a journey plumber over at least two years..
  • Plumbing Contractor License: the business license, which requires a master plumber to represent the company..
  • Use LARA's online license lookup to check a name or a business before work begins..

What a first call covers

The person who answers asks where the water is, what you have already tried and whether anyone is at risk. If water is spreading, you get shutoff steps right away. Then we talk through what is likely wrong, what the job usually involves and what it affects in price.

You do not need the right plumbing words. Describing a sound, a smell or a stain is enough. Photos help if you can text them later, but they are not required to get a plan.

How we price and explain work

Price depends on the problem, the access and the parts. We give you the scope and the clear price before work starts, so you decide with the facts in hand. If the job turns out to be bigger once a wall or floor is open, we stop and explain before going further.

We do not promise arrival times. We tell you what we know when you call, and we say so plainly when a same-day visit is not available.

What to have ready when you call

Know where your main water shutoff is, and have the street address and a callback number handy. Tell us if anyone is elderly, if there is a power outlet near the water, and whether the house is a rental. Landlords can also tell us who holds the keys.

For drains, note which fixtures are affected. For water heaters, note whether the unit is gas or electric and how old it is if you know. Small details save time on the phone and help us bring the right parts for the job.
