When a plumbing problem is an emergency

Some problems can wait until morning and some cannot. A pipe that is spraying, sewage coming up through a floor drain, a water heater leaking from the tank body, or water running toward an electrical panel is an emergency. The first move is the same in every case: stop the water if you can, keep people and pets away from the wet area, and call our 24/7 line.

A dripping faucet, a slow drain or a toilet that runs between flushes is different. Those are worth fixing soon, and we schedule them for the next same-day or next-day opening, but they do not need a midnight call.

If you are unsure which side of the line you are on, call and ask. The person on the phone will tell you honestly whether it can wait.

Four things to do before the plumber arrives

Close the main valve.

Turn the house shutoff clockwise until it stops. In most homes it sits where the water line enters, often near the water heater or the meter.

Keep clear of wet electrics.

If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, stay out and turn off power only if you can reach the breaker while standing dry.

Stop using drains.

For a backup, no flushing, no dishwasher, no laundry. Every drop you add goes to the lowest opening.

Take photos.

A few phone photos of the damage help with an insurance claim later and take thirty seconds.

Plumbing for a growing college town

Allendale is home to the Grand Valley State University campus, and the township has grown quickly: its population rose from 20,708 in 2010 to 26,582 in 2020 according to the township Master Plan. A large share of that growth is new construction and rental housing, so many of the homes we visit are under twenty years old.

That makes the work different from an old city. There are fewer corroded pipes and more fixture failures, water heater problems, clogs from heavy use and plumbing in rental houses that change tenants often.

If you manage a rental near campus, a quick call after a tenant reports a slow drain or a running toilet usually costs far less than the water damage that follows a missed one. Lake Michigan Drive (M-45) carries most of the commercial traffic through the township, and we also work on restaurants, offices and apartment buildings along it.

Water, sewer and where we fit

Allendale Charter Township supplies municipal water bought from the City of Grand Rapids, which draws from Lake Michigan, and runs its own sewer treatment facility. Rural parts of the township use private wells and septic systems regulated through the Ottawa County Health Department.

We work on the plumbing inside your property and the service lines serving it. For problems in the municipal main under the street, the township utility posts its own contact details on its water and sewer page. If you are not sure whose problem it is, call us and we will help you work out the next step.

Calls from Standale and Grand Rapids

We also take calls from Standale and from Grand Rapids. Both are a longer drive than Allendale itself, so timing depends on the job and on where our technicians are, and we will tell you plainly what is possible when you call.

Frequently asked questions

Is your emergency plumber really available 24/7 in Allendale?

Yes. The phone line is answered around the clock, every day of the year. Urgent problems such as burst pipes and sewage backups are handled as soon as a technician is available, and you get a first step by phone straight away.

What counts as a plumbing emergency?

Active water you cannot stop, sewage coming up through drains, a leaking water heater tank, no water at all, or a gas smell near a gas appliance. For gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

How much will it cost?

The price depends on the problem, the access and the parts. We explain the scope and the clear price before work starts, so you decide with the full picture. See the services page for what drives cost on each type of job.

What should I do while I wait for the plumber?

Close the main water valve if water is the problem, stop using the affected drains, keep away from wet electrical areas and take photos for your insurer. Our phone team will add steps specific to your situation.

Do you offer same-day appointments?

Same-day appointments are offered when they are available. Urgent problems go first. If a same-day slot is not open, we tell you the next opening instead of leaving you guessing.

Which towns do you serve besides Allendale?

We serve Jenison, Hudsonville, Coopersville, Marne and Walker, plus calls from Standale and Grand Rapids.

Are you licensed and insured?

Yes, we are licensed and insured. Michigan licenses plumbers through the state's Bureau of Construction Codes, and you can check any plumber's license on the state's online license lookup before you hire them.

Do you handle landlords and rental properties?

Yes. Rental houses and apartments around Allendale are a regular part of our work. Call from the property and tell us it is a rental so we can plan access with the tenant.
