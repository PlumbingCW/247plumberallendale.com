Underground line leaks: three signs to watch for

A buried line leaks out of sight, so the clues show up in the yard, at the meter and at the tap. Here is how they usually appear.

A wet spot or sunken yard.

Grass that stays green and soggy in a dry spell, a patch that gives under your foot, or a slight dip in the lawn along the line between the meter and the house. In winter, look for ice or melted snow where the rest of the yard is frozen.

A meter that spins with fixtures off.

Shut off every tap and appliance and watch the meter head. If it shows flow, close the main inside the house and look again. A meter that keeps moving points to the buried section.

Low pressure through the whole house.

A leak that is large enough can drag pressure down at every tap, because water is escaping before it reaches the building. Note whether the drop came on suddenly or over weeks.

Active leak.

Water surfacing in the yard right now? First moves

  1. Close the main shutoff valve inside the house. If water keeps surfacing, the leak is upstream of that valve, so tell us..
  2. Do not dig or probe the wet patch. Lines are not the only buried things, and you may hit a gas line, a cable or a private electric run..
  3. Keep kids and pets away from soft, sunken ground and standing water near the meter..
  4. Call the 24/7 line and describe the spot, the meter reading and whether the pressure changed overnight..

Low water pressure: service line, fixture or regulator?

Low pressure has several causes, and only some are underground. This table separates them in a few minutes with no tools.

One tap is weak, others are normal.A clogged aerator or a failed cartridge at that fixture.Remove the aerator and rinse it, or see our faucet repair page.

How low pressure gets diagnosed

A pressure gauge on an outdoor hose bib tells us what the house is actually receiving. A steady reading that is low points upstream, at the regulator or the line. A reading that is fine at the hose bib and low at a single sink points back to that fixture.

A regulator is a bell-shaped valve near the main shutoff. When its internal spring fails, pressure can fall, spike or creep upward overnight. It is a common fix, and worth ruling out before anyone talks about digging the yard. If a meter test points underground, leak detection locates the leak before any dig.

Yard and meter-box leaks

The line often fails close to where it enters the meter box or near a connector, and the clue is water collecting inside the box lid. Do not reach into a box with water in it if you can see a wire or if the lid is cracked. Tell us the box is wet and we will plan the fix.

A split in an exposed section is a burst pipe repair, and a line that has failed repeatedly may call for repiping. We also cover Hudsonville callers to the south.

Call MISS DIG 811 before anyone digs

Michigan's one-call system asks you to contact MISS DIG 811 at least 3 business days before any digging, for any project regardless of size. The call is free. Public utility lines are marked. Private lines, such as a yard light feed or a buried sprinkler line, are not marked.

We will tell you when a dig is required and what has to be located first. If you hire a contractor, ask whether they have called, and check that your ticket is still valid on the day.

Allendale, MI: township water, a lead inventory and where the township's role ends

Allendale Charter Township buys its water from the City of Grand Rapids, whose supply comes from Lake Michigan. The township's 2025 water quality report states a service line inventory of 3,979 lines, with zero known lead or presumed lead service lines. That is the township's inventory, and it is not a test of your tap.

Ask the township utility where its responsibility ends and yours begins, before you assume who pays for a repair. Our visit can then focus on the part that is yours. If you are outside the township water system and use a private well, this page does not apply. Ask Ottawa County about your well and testing.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water service line emergencies at night or on weekends in Allendale?

Yes. The line is open 24/7. At night we focus on stopping the loss, confirming where the leak is and tying down a plan, and a full dig is scheduled once MISS DIG 811 has been called.

Who owns which part of the line, me or the township?

We do not guess at that. Ask the township utility where its responsibility ends. Once you know, we can price the part that is yours and explain how it works with the township's side.

Does MISS DIG 811 cost anything?

No. The call or online request is free and is needed at least 3 business days before digging. The utilities mark their lines. Private lines on your property, such as sprinkler feeds, are not marked.

Does the township say it has lead service lines?

The township's 2025 water quality report states 3,979 service lines in its inventory with zero known lead or presumed lead lines. It is the township's inventory. If you want to know what material enters your house, we can look at the pipe where it comes through the wall.

Why is my water pressure low?

It could be a clogged fixture, a failing regulator or a leaking service line. A meter test and a pressure gauge on an outdoor tap usually tell us which, before any digging is discussed.

I have a private well. Can you help?

This page covers lines connected to the township water system. For wells, testing and well-related rules, start with the Ottawa County Health Department.

Will the lawn be dug up?

Only as much as the repair needs. We locate the leak first to keep the opening tight, and we explain what will be disturbed and what restoration will involve before any work begins.
