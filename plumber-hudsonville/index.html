Who answers when a Hudsonville sewer backs up on a Saturday night?

The city's public works line for sewer back-ups is staffed Monday through Thursday during the day and on Friday morning. For every other hour, weekends and holidays included, the city's sewer page says to call 911. That guidance is about the public sewer. The pipe from your house to the street is usually yours, and clearing it is a plumber's job.

The same page carries a "Don't Rush to Flush" list, which matters more as hundreds of new rental units join the system. If a toilet and a tub back up together, the line that serves the whole house is blocked, and drain cleaning on that main run is the first call.

What does a 24-hour meter reading tell you about a hidden leak?

The city's water page says meters usually sit in a basement or utility closet, and that a meter shows a LEAK flag after 24 hours of continuous flow. If no fixture is running and that flag is lit, water is escaping somewhere past the meter.

Shut every tap and note the dial, then close the main and look again. If the dial stops only with the main closed, the loss is inside the house and leak detection can pin down where. If it keeps turning, the problem may sit on the street side, which is the city's to fix.

Rush Creek, Chicago Drive and your sump pit

Rush Creek runs through Hudsonville's central residential streets, and the master plan marks "muck" land along Chicago Drive in the northeast corner as floodplain. We found no reported flood event for the city, so this is about readiness, not history. A pump that has sat idle since last spring deserves a bucket test before the thaw.

Pour water into the pit until the float rises, and listen for the pump to start and the pit to empty. If it hums, stalls or never shuts off, sump pump replacement is the next step.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency service available in Hudsonville?

Yes. The 24/7 line is staffed every night, weekend and holiday, and a plumber is sent from Allendale. Give the dispatcher your street and say whether the water is off. For a gas smell, get outside and call 911 before anything else.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Hudsonville?

We cover 49426, the city's ZIP, which also serves homes and schools in neighboring parts of Georgetown and Jamestown townships, such as the Port Sheldon Road and 48th Avenue areas. We also cover 49428, the Jenison ZIP lying directly north of the city. Your street address decides the dispatch, not the ZIP.

How far is Hudsonville from your Allendale base?

About 10 miles and about 20 minutes by our estimate. The mapped route runs 56th Avenue, Fillmore Street and 36th Avenue, then Balsam Drive and 32nd Avenue. Traffic changes that, so there is no arrival promise.

Is Hudsonville water hard?

The city's 2025 water report lists a hardness average of 98.78, with a range of 91.1 to 118.8. No unit is printed on that line, but the report defines its units as mg/L, which would be about 5.8 grains per gallon on average by our conversion. A test strip at your own tap is the surest check.

How do I tell a street main break from a leak in my house?

In 2025 a construction company hit a Hudsonville water main and closed Port Sheldon Street between Wilshire Drive and Sanru Avenue, according to FOX 17. If neighbors lose pressure too, call the city. If only your house is wet or your meter keeps turning with the main closed, call us.

Who pulls plumbing permits in Hudsonville?

The city's permits page links its plumbing permit to Professional Code Inspections, whose Jenison office is at 1913 Baldwin St. For fees or inspection steps, ask that office.

I rent in a new Hudsonville building. Who do I call?

Close the shutoff and tell your property manager first. If you cannot reach them and water is still running, call us and say it is a rental. We explain the scope and the price before work starts.

Does the city care about my sprinkler or softener plumbing?

The city's water page says it must find and prevent cross connections under state rules, and it lists lawn irrigation, water softeners, fire sprinklers, hot tubs, boilers and toilet fill valves among residential examples. Ask the public works office what it expects before you add or change one.
