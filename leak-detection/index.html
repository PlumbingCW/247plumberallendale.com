Water spreading right now? Do these four things first

  1. Close the main shutoff valve where the water line enters the house. A lever handle is closed when it sits crosswise to the pipe..
  2. Switch off power to wet rooms at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it without standing in water. If the panel is wet, stay away and tell us..
  3. Lift rugs, move valuables and set buckets under drips. Photograph the damage and the meter before you clean, since a landlord or insurer will ask..
  4. Call the 24/7 line and say where you first saw water and whether it stops once the main is closed. That one fact narrows the search a great deal..

High water bill or a spinning meter? Test it yourself first

A bill that creeps up with no change in habits is the classic sign of a leak nobody can see. Allendale's own 2025 water quality report offers a simple check worth doing before you pay for a visit. Start with every faucet and appliance off, including the ice maker and any humidifier.

Then lift the meter lid and look at the meter head. On a newer all-plastic meter, a plus sign in the circle means water is moving. On an older brass meter, watch the small triangle, and if it is turning, water is flowing. Flow with everything off means the leak is somewhere past the meter.

Run the look twice, because a softener recharge can fool one glance. Then close the main valve inside the house and look again. A meter that stops means the leak is indoors. A meter that keeps moving points to the buried pipe, which becomes a water service line repair job. The same report names two usual suspects: a dripping faucet, and a toilet flapper that seals only some of the time.

Slab leak signs: warm spots, a running meter and a hissing floor

A slab leak is a pressurized line leaking beneath a concrete floor. It rarely makes a puddle at first, so the clues are quiet.

Warm or hot spots.

A patch of floor that feels warm underfoot, or hot water that takes longer to arrive, can mean a leaking hot line below.

A meter that will not rest.

With every fixture off, a meter that keeps showing flow and a bill that has climbed are the most reliable signs.

Sound.

In a quiet house at night, a faint hiss or running water from the floor deserves a listening visit.

Slab leaks: what happens once it is found

We locate the leak first, then explain the choices. Sometimes the line can be rerouted through the walls or attic and the concrete left alone. Sometimes the slab is opened over the exact spot. If the whole line is tired, our repiping page explains when replacing it beats chasing one spot at a time.

Ceiling and wall leaks: why the stain is rarely under the pipe

Water travels along joists, studs and pipes before it shows, so a brown ring on the dining room ceiling can sit several feet from the source. Cutting a hole where the stain is often finds nothing.

A stain that grows only while someone showers points to a drain or shower pan. One that grows with nothing running points to a supply line. One that appears only after rain may be a roof problem, which is outside what we do, and we will say so rather than open your ceiling. A moisture meter and a thermal camera let the opening be the size of a hand.

Leaks under the house: crawlspace and basement ceiling lines

Where supply pipes run in the open, look for a sagging wet strip of insulation, rust streaks on pipe hangers, a musty smell, or drips you can hear from the floor above. Winter raises the stakes, because a small weep can become a split pipe after a hard freeze.

Do not crawl into a space with standing water near wiring. Tell us what you can see from the access hatch and we will plan the safest way in. A split line moves the job to burst pipe repair.

How we find a leak without tearing things open

Listening equipment picks up the sound water makes escaping a pressurized pipe, through a floor, wall or the ground. Moisture meters and thermal cameras read dampness and temperature differences. Pressure tests close off a section of pipe and watch a gauge to see if it holds.

None of these finds every leak. A very slow weep behind tile or a line under thick concrete can narrow to an area, not a point. We say so plainly, and talk through whether to keep searching or reroute the line.

Sprinkler and irrigation leaks

A zone that stays soggy, weak spray at the heads and a summer bill that jumps are the signs. Shut the irrigation supply off at its own valve and repeat the meter test. If the meter stops, the leak is on the sprinkler side. After a hard winter, cracked fittings often show only when the system is turned on in spring.

Pool and spa lines, and leaks in drain pipes

For a pool or spa we look at the plumbing side only: fill lines, equipment pad piping and valves. A bucket on a step, compared with the pool level over a day, separates evaporation from real loss. Liner and shell leaks belong to a pool company.

A drain pipe leak is quieter. Think slow drains, a sewer smell, a damp spot under a tub or gnats at a floor drain. A camera run, as on our sewer line inspection page, shows cracks and offset joints.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle leak detection emergencies at night or on weekends in Allendale?

Yes. The line is staffed 24/7, and a leak with water actively spreading goes to the front of the schedule. Close the main valve, switch off power to wet rooms if it is safe, and we take it from there.

How do I know if my high water bill is caused by a leak?

Turn everything off and read the meter head. A plus sign on a plastic meter or a turning triangle on a brass one means water is flowing. If it stays on with the house main closed, the leak is likely in the buried line.

Can you find a leak without cutting into the wall or floor?

Usually we can narrow it down with listening gear, moisture meters, thermal imaging and pressure tests before any opening is made. The opening is then limited to where the pipe actually is.

Will you always find the leak?

No method finds every leak. A very slow weep or a line under thick concrete may narrow to an area instead of a point. We tell you what we found, how sure we are and what the options are.

I rent in Allendale. Who should call about a leak?

Tell your landlord or property manager first, since they usually decide who works on the building. A manager can call us directly with the unit address and what the tenant reported.

Is a slab leak always a major repair?

Not always. Some are fixed by rerouting the line overhead. Others need the slab opened. You hear both options, with a clear price, before any work starts.
