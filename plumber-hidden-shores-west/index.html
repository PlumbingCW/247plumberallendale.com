A planned development built in phases

Township planning commission minutes from 2025 describe Hidden Shores West as a Planned Unit Development approved to allow different densities. In early 2025 the commission took up Phase 5, a site condominium of 49 single-family homes described as the final phase of the project, and agreed to move it to a public hearing on March 3, 2025.

The same minutes record that a 2016 amendment removed a planned berm along 78th Avenue while keeping the trees. The plan sheets for Phase 5 note that utilities will be buried.

Real-estate listings place homes on Shorewood Street and Poppy Lane in the community, and a separate group of condominiums nearby uses the name Water's Edge. We treat those as supporting detail, because a listing is not a township record.

Why house age varies by street

A development finished over many years does not have one plumbing age. A water heater installed with the first phase has run for years, while one in a Phase 5 house has barely started. When you call, say which street you live on and roughly when the house was built.

Listings we reviewed describe public water and public sewer for a Hidden Shores West home, and others describe walk-out or daylight basements. We cannot promise that applies to your address, so confirm utilities on your bill.

Common reasons to call in a newer lakeside development

These are general reasons, not a claim that homes here have a particular defect.

A water heater past its warranty.

Many tank heaters carry warranties of a few years to a decade. If yours is near the end, compare repair with replacement or a tankless unit before it fails.

A sump pump that has never been tested.

A below-grade or walk-out level can collect groundwater after heavy rain or a thaw. Lift the float, confirm the pump starts and drains, and read about sump pump service.

A slow drain or a running toilet.

These are ordinary wear in a house of any age. Toilet repair and drain cleaning cover the usual causes.

Township water, township sewer, and who to ask

Allendale Charter Township buys its water from the City of Grand Rapids and treats sewage at its own plant. The township lists its utility contact at 616-895-6295 for service and billing, with separate numbers for emergencies on its water and sewer page.

Plumbing permits and inspections go through Professional Code Inspections. If your association has rules about exterior work or irrigation, check them before you plan a project.

Sources: Allendale Charter Township water and sewer page, planning and zoning page, Planning Commission 2025 minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Is Hidden Shores West a subdivision or a condominium?

Township minutes call it a Planned Unit Development, and its later phases are site condominiums of single-family homes. A nearby group named Water's Edge is a separate set of condominiums.

How many homes are in Hidden Shores West?

We could not find a total. The minutes say the final phase adds 49 single-family units. We will not guess the whole count.

Who handles water and sewer problems?

Inside your home, a plumber handles them. For a problem in the street, call the township utility office at 616-895-6295. If a boil notice or outage affects the block, the township will say so.

Should I check my association before a plumbing project?

Yes for anything outside the walls, such as irrigation or a visible sump discharge. Interior repairs rarely need association approval, but your documents decide.
