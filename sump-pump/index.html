Allendale, MI: the Grand River, wetlands and your parcel

The Grand River forms the township's northern and eastern boundaries, and the township Master Plan notes that much adjacent land lies in the FEMA 100-year floodplain. The Bass River basin and its wetlands sit in the northwest. We are not saying homes here flood. We are saying that the water table near the river is a real factor for a basement.

So check your own parcel on the FEMA flood map, and ask the county or the township what it shows for your address. If your lot is near the river or the wetlands, a battery backup and a high-water alarm earn their cost much faster than they do on high ground.

Battery backup, check valve and where the water goes

A backup system is a second pump on its own battery, so a power cut during a storm does not stop the pit from emptying. A high-water alarm is the cheapest add-on, because it tells you before the water reaches the floor.

The check valve sits on the discharge pipe just above the pump. It keeps the water that has been pumped out from running back into the pit. When it fails, the pump short cycles and wears out early. A valve that clunks loudly on each cycle usually needs replacing.

Where the discharge ends matters. It should carry water well away from the foundation, so it does not circle back into the pit. A buried discharge line means digging, so call MISS DIG 811 at least 3 business days ahead. Whether sump water may go to the street or storm drain is a local rule, so ask the township.

Installing a sump pump: the basics

A sump basin is a lined pit in the lowest part of the floor, with a pump seated on a firm base. The pump needs its own grounded outlet, a float that can swing without touching the pit wall, a check valve and a discharge pipe that exits the house with a downward slope.

Size matters. A pump that is too small runs nonstop in a heavy week, and one that is too large short cycles and burns out sooner. We look at how much water the pit actually receives before recommending a model. If the existing pit is cracked or silted, replacing the pump alone is not enough.

Sump pumps versus ejector pumps

A sump pump moves groundwater and storm water. An ejector pump moves wastewater from a basement bathroom, laundry or utility sink up to the sewer line. The two look similar but are not interchangeable. An ejector sits in a sealed, vented basin and handles solids.

Both fail the same way: a float that sticks, a switch that wears, a motor that burns out. Both also fail at bad moments. If a sewage smell comes with a pump alarm, call right away and keep the basement closed off. Sewer-side clogs that back up into a basement are handled by sewer line cleaning.

If the pit is cracked or the floor keeps seeping, see basement waterproofing, and a new pit and outlet fall under plumbing installation. We serve Marne callers too.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle sump pump emergencies at night or on weekends in Allendale?

Yes. Our line is open 24/7, and a failed pump during a storm is a priority call. While you wait, keep clear of wet outlets, move belongings up and bail the pit if it is safe.

Why does my sump pump run all the time?

The common causes are a stuck float, a failed check valve letting water fall back in, a pump that is too small, or a very high water table in a wet week. We test the float and valve first.

My pump runs but the pit stays full. What now?

Check the discharge. A clog, a kinked line or a frozen outlet stops water from leaving. If that is clear, the impeller may be jammed or the pump worn out.

Do I need a battery backup?

A backup makes sense if a power cut during a storm would flood the basement. It matters more on low ground or near the river. We explain what a backup adds before you decide.

Is my Allendale home in the floodplain?

The township Master Plan says much land next to the Grand River is in the FEMA 100-year floodplain. Look up your parcel on the FEMA flood map and ask the county or township about your address.

What is the difference between a sump pump and an ejector pump?

A sump pump removes groundwater from the pit. An ejector pumps wastewater from basement fixtures to the sewer line, in a sealed basin. They are different pumps with different parts.

Where should the sump discharge go?

Away from the foundation, on a slope, with the outlet clear. Rules about street or storm drain discharge are local, so ask the township. If we bury a line, MISS DIG 811 must be called first.
