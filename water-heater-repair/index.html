Water heater leak: where it comes from and what to shut off first

Most leaks around a tank are not the tank. Trace the water upward from the floor. The usual sources are the drain valve at the bottom, the temperature and pressure relief valve with its discharge pipe, and the hot and cold connections on top. All of these are repairable parts.

A relief valve that drips is telling you something. It opens when pressure or temperature climbs too high, so a steady drip can point to a worn valve, high household pressure or thermal expansion. Swapping the valve without finding the cause often means the new one drips too. Never cap the discharge pipe.

Water from the tank body is different. Seepage at the bottom seam, under the jacket or with rust streaks down the side means the inner steel has corroded through. That cannot be patched, and it is the point where we move to water heater replacement. Shut off first: the cold supply valve, then the breaker or the gas control. If the water turns out to come from a pipe instead, that is a burst pipe call.

Gas smell, or a carbon monoxide alarm: leave first

If you smell gas near the tank, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. Do not relight a pilot, flip switches or look for the source. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave and call 911. We handle the water side and the repair once the house is safe.

No hot water in the house right now? Sort out which problem it is

No hot water at all usually means lost power or fuel, a tripped breaker, a dead element or thermostat on an electric tank, or a pilot that has gone out on a gas one. Lukewarm water suggests one failed element, a weak flame or a broken dip tube. Hot water that vanishes after a few minutes can mean sediment, a dead lower element or a tank that has aged out.

A cold January morning makes a marginal heater look worse, because the incoming water is colder and the heater has more to make up. If your tank has been slow all week, do not wait for it to quit. Call any hour and we will tell you whether it is a repair or time to look at the next tank, perhaps a tankless unit.

What you can check, and what to leave to a plumber

Safe to check yourself

  • Is the breaker tripped, or is the gas control set to its run or pilot position?.
  • Is the cold supply valve fully open?.
  • Does only one tap run cold? That points to a fixture or mixing valve, not the heater..
  • Is the puddle from the tank, or from a pipe, fixture or condensation above it?.

Leave these to us

  • Opening electrical panels or testing elements and thermostats.
  • Anything on the gas side, including repeated pilot failures and the flue.
  • Replacing a relief valve or changing the discharge pipe.
  • Steam or very hot water at the discharge pipe: stay clear and call.

What affects a water heater repair bill

A clear price comes before the work. We do not print dollar figures here because repairs range from a small valve to a major part, and these five things decide where yours falls.

Which part failed.

A thermocouple or element is a smaller job than a gas control valve or a relief valve with a pressure problem behind it.

Fuel and type.

Gas, electric and hybrid units have different parts, different safety checks and different labor.

Age and condition of the tank.

On an old tank we may advise against a repair that costs a large share of a new unit.

Access and fittings.

Corroded unions, seized shutoffs or a cramped closet add time.

The cause behind the symptom.

A dripping relief valve may need an expansion tank or pressure fix as well, and we price that openly.

Insurance, honestlyA repair for a worn part is usually a maintenance cost, while sudden water damage may be treated differently under a homeowner policy. We cannot tell you what yours covers, so ask your insurer before deciding on anything large.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water heater repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Allendale?

Yes. We answer 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays. A leaking tank gets same-day attention when our schedule allows, and you are told what to shut off while you wait.

Why is my water heater leaking from the bottom?

A drip at the bottom can be a loose drain valve, which is a quick repair. If water seeps from the seam or from beneath the jacket, the tank has corroded through and needs replacing.

Is a dripping relief valve pipe an emergency?

A slow drip is urgent but not dangerous in itself. Steam or a steady stream of very hot water is a reason to stay clear and call right away. Never cap or plug the pipe.

My pilot light keeps going out. What is wrong?

Common causes are a worn thermocouple, a dirty pilot assembly or a draft in the flue. If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911 first. Otherwise call us and do not keep relighting it.

How do I know if a repair is worth it?

Age, the part that failed and the number of recent repairs decide it. We will tell you if a repair is a poor buy.

What if the water is coming from a pipe, not the heater?

Then the problem is the plumbing rather than the tank, and our burst pipe repair service handles active leaks. Tell us what you see and we will sort it out on the phone.
